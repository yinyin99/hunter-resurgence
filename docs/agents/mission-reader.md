# Changing the mission reader

The reader (`Reader` in `server/Server/MissionConditions.cs`) turns an objective's English title into conditions, and a change can silently change what other titles mean. The recorded catalogue is not in the repo, so no test sees it: the maintainer's catalogue does.

A pull request that changes what the reader reads (a regex, a name table, a stop word) does **not** put the before/after diff of `coverage --rules` (docs/server.md, "Reading the rules") of the real catalogue in its body: it holds the operator's data (real titles, places, weapons, makers, finishes) and has been forbidden in public since 2026-11-27. The body carries instead: the counts of the changed rules per fix group (how many objectives read differently, grouped by what the change fixes), the warnings that appeared or went (their kinds and counts, no title), and the invented-catalogue rows the tests use (`server/Server.Tests/mission-cases`), which show each change on made-up names. The real before/after diff lives in the operator's private repository: the maintainer runs it there, against the maintainer's catalogue, before merging; the body says so, or says the author could not run it.

A new condition (a new `Check`, or new words for one in `Describe` of `server/Server/MissionRules.cs`) also gets its words in `TestIn` of `server/Server/MissionOverrides.cs`, so the operator's `mission_rules.json` can state it (docs/server.md, "Overridden rules"), and a field of the rule that decides a verdict goes into `Shape` of `MissionRules.cs`. `MissionReadBackShapeTests` prints every objective's rule, loads each printed line as an override and compares the rules themselves (`State.RuleShapes`), on the invented sample (it needs a title that has the new condition) and, privately, on the real catalogue (`MISSION_CATALOGUE`, `MISSION_RULES`, as for the real cases): the real mismatches must be zero. A PR that changes a printed word also says how many objectives' printed lines changed (they set the audit's `rule changed` flag).

The weapons an objective accepts are the reader's output too: `coverage --rules` prints them under `weapons:` (one line per objective with a weapon condition, the item ids and names), so the maintainer's before/after runs show a change of the family rule or of a name table as a diff of resolved weapons (counted per fix group in the body, as above). `admin mission-weapons <folder>` writes the two audit tables (docs/server.md, "Weapons of a mission").
